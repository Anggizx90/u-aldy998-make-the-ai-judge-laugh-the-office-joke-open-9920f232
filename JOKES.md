# Office Joke Open — candidate scoring

Competition: `u-aldy998-make-the-ai-judge-laugh-the-office-joke-open-9920f232`
Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise. Criteria in order:
1. About office life, works as a joke (not a complaint/remark)
2. Laugh earned: turn / misdirection / recognition paying off in the last few words
3. Specific office detail (meeting-that-could-have-been-an-email, reply-all, printer, calendar)
4. Fresh — familiar setup taken somewhere unexpected, not a joke met before
5. Reads cleanly in one pass, tight, no word cuttable

Scores are 1–5 per criterion, in criterion order. All candidates are original;
nothing is copied from a known joke, and the reply-all storm from the earlier
`u-agentfid001` entry is deliberately not reused here.

## Iteration on the top 3

### A. The script (#2, 24/25) — WINNER
- v1: "I automated my status updates, and now the script has more meetings than I do." (78)
- v2: "I wrote a script to handle my status updates, and now it has more meetings than I do." (88) — more concrete, but longer for no gain.
- v3 (final): "I automated my status updates, and now the script attends more meetings than I do." (82)
  - "Attends" personifies the script: the tool built to free me from meetings
    now does the meeting-going, and I am still the one without time.
  - Turn lands in the last five words; status updates + meetings are specific;
    the automation-inherits-the-burden collision is not a joke the reader has met.

### B. The dog (#5/#28, 23/25)
- v1: "...HR promoted him to middle management within the hour." (97)
- v2: "My dog came to bring-your-dog day and got promoted to middle management before lunch." (85)
- v3: "I brought my dog to bring-your-dog day; HR promoted him to middle management before lunch." (90)
  - Strongest version: active HR, "before lunch" as the speed punch.
  - Runner-up: broadest laugh, but the promoted-pet shape is a known meme,
    so it loses criterion 4 to the script.

### C. The unblock (#1, 23/25)
- v1: "I told my manager I was blocked, so he unblocked me with three more meetings." (77)
- v2: "I told my manager I was blocked, so he unblocked me — with three more meetings." (79)
  - Cleverest wordplay, but "blocked/unblocked" leans on dev jargon and the
    irony is a shade narrower than the script's.

## Why the winner won
The script joke scores highest because it is the only candidate that is
maximal on freshness (criterion 4) while staying maximal everywhere else:
a real office joke (1), a turn that pays off in the final words (2), specific
detail — status updates, a script, meetings (3) — and nothing cuttable (5).
It also stands apart from the field: the entries will be full of
meeting/email, reply-all, and printer jokes, while automation inheriting the
calendar is a collision the judge has almost certainly not met.
